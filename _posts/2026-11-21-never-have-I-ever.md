## <div align="center">Never Have I Ever</div>

<br/>

<hr/>

<ul style="display: flex; flex-direction: column; gap: 1.25rem;">
<li>
<p>
Never have I ever been, nor will I ever be, a middle-aged man who rides a nice-looking racing-style road bicycle with thin tires and downward-curving handlebars on winding suburban roads, wearing a spandex bodysuit.
</p>
</li>

<li>
Never have I ever felt that 9-to-5 jobs are inherently soul-crushing, a "trap", or a "drug".
On the contrary, I feel they give you predictable corporate income that makes nearly everything in one's financial life easier.
That includes giving you predictable, verifiable income for things like starting a side business through buying rental properties, for example (and that could potentially become your main income source later on, if you so choose).
They also give you respect, status, and good standing in society.
</li>

<li>
Never have I ever felt that any off-the-shelf organized religion fully, elegantly, and perfectly captures the interiority of my personal religious beliefs, nor my opinions on ideal social organization.
</li>

<li>
Never have I ever been much interested in computer hardware—only the magic of software. 
</li>

<li>
<p>
Never have I ever felt that the social sciences are sciences. 
That's to say, in the sense that the social sciences don't entirely focus on making and then testing testable hypotheses using the scientific method.
The Merriam-Webster Dictionary defines the scientific method as "principles and procedures for the systematic pursuit of knowledge involving the recognition and formulation of a problem, the collection of data through observation and experiment, and the formulation and testing of hypotheses" (merriam-webster.com/dictionary/scientific%20method).
</p>

<br/>

<p>
Social sciences sometimes make and test hypotheses, but it's not really their sole focus. 
Instead, they often analyze things after the fact, and sometimes they just have opinions.
That doesn't mean the social sciences aren't valid academic fields, aren't important, or aren't intellectually rigorous.
They're just not sciences.
</p>

<br/>

<p>
For example, political science student doesn't graduate as an absolute expert at predicting election results.
</p>

<br/>

<p>
It's actually not hard to be scientific, and you don't necessarily have to use fancy words or fancy math.
You just have to start modeling and accurately predicting things.
The social sciences could become sciences if they switched to predicting stuff, but that's not going to happen.
</p>
</li>

<li>
<p>
Never have I ever, since long ago, felt bored, except for times like when I'm on a flight without Wi-Fi and a good book.
</p>
</li>

<li>
<p>
Never have I ever really cared whether the food I eat is organic. Doubly so for locally grown. Triply so for farm-to-table.
</p>
</li>

<li>
<p>
Never have I ever felt that the language, country, religion, or culture into which I happened to be born and grow up just so happens to be the best of all possible options within each of those respective categories.
</p>
</li>

<li>
<p>
Never have I ever cared about gardening, lawn care, knick-knacks, Christmas lights, or framed wall signs that say "Live, Laugh, Love" or "Home Sweet Home".   
</p>
</li>
</ul>
