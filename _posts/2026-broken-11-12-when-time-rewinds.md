<hr/>

<p>
I once read a poem by the poet Wesley McNair (who as an aside is from the small town that I'm from in Maine) about human flourishing in adulthood.
</p>

<p>
In the poem, McNair uses the visual metaphor of a wristwatch. 
He talks about being an adult looking at his wristwatch and (metaphorically) seeing the hands of the watch run backward.
The idea is he's getting back the time he "sat through" in his youth.
To me, it's not necessarily that the time in youth was bad or wasted, just that it had a different quality to it.
</p>

<p>
Think of watching the wall clock while you sit through a class in high school, counting the minutes and glancing out the window every so often.
Part of the difference is we have more freedom in adulthood. We can choose our job, where we live, and so on.
But to me, there's also an aspect of the life of the mind, which can even start in childhood, although perhaps then one is functionally an adult in some way.
Perhaps we should consider a person an adult, in the sense of affording them the status and respect that comes with adulthood, as when a person has found a pro-social passion in life.
</p>

<p>
For me, that backwards-flowing temporality often lives in degree of proximity to the life of the mind, through subjects like these:
</p>
<ul>
    <li>The Riemann hypothesis</li>
    <li>Retroviral vector-based gene therapy</li>
    <li>American re-industrialization</li>
    <li>Biohacking, trans-humanism, longevity, cryonics, and immortality</li>
    <li>Sub-nucleonic structure (what's inside a quark?)</li>
    <li>Optimal socio-political-economic structuring (Norway?)</li>
    <li>Quantum electrodynamics, and more fascinatingly, quantum chromodynamics</li>
    <li>NP-completeness</li>
    <li>Gödel incompleteness</li>
    <li>Tuberculosis vaccines</li>
    <li>Condensed plain-language complete-certainty answers to all the important theological questions. Especially and in particular, the answer to the question of theodicy: why does God permit the existence of evil?</li>
    <li>What Dr. Paul Farmer called "structural violence" (deciding between food and medicine, for example), the preconditions of war, and unequal access to health care</li>
    <li>Quantum gravity</li>
    <li>Big Bang cosmology: nucleosynthesis, inflationary cosmology, cosmic anisotropy, and baryogenesis</li>
</ul>
