Add the space stations, moon landings, probes mass to orbit, excellent music like Stevie Wonder, Hollywood movies, some great literature, virtually all of the world's largest and most impactful companies, modern democracy, winning both world wars and the Cold war and the Korean war (saving the world from some crazy stuff many times over), and so on

The United States is a good cultural fit for people with a spirit of adventure, exploration, freedom, informality, individualism, innovation, and risk-taking.
I was born and raised here in the US and yet it's not a great fit for me culturally. That's why I moved to Europe, and I'll be moving back soon. If you're collectivistic, consensus-oriented, comfort-oriented, and above all, safety-oriented, somewhere like the Nordics would be a better fit. When I visited Sweden, I felt the most at peace I've ever felt.
On the other hands, Americans invented AC electricity, automobiles, airplanes, antibiotics, semiconductors, integrated circuits, superconductivity, modern computers, UNIX, C, most of quantum electrodynamics, the internet, GPS, smartphones, the only treatment that works for HIV/AIDS (the antiretroviral cocktail), Google, 8 of the 14 standard childhood vaccinations given worldwide (the ones of polio, measles, mumps, rubella, haemophilus influenzae type b, pneumococcal, and rotavirus), and virtually all current artificial intelligence.
Can you name anything important discovered or invented by Bulgaria, Belarus, Bosnia, Latvia, Lithuania, Lichtenstein, Luxembourg, Malta, Moldova, or Monaco?
At the same time, if you're looking for a mostly sane, stable, peaceful, reasonable, agreeable society, you're barking up the wrong tree in the United States.
And so I have a potentially unusual opinion on the whole matter, which is:
a) it's good there exists at least one place like the United States
b) but I don't want to live there
